WARDEN
Executive Summary Doc WDN-ES-01 Prepared for Government & Public-Sector Partners
A proposal for public-sector deployment

Bring useful AI to everyone — without sending anyone's life to the cloud.

Warden is a home and institutional AI that runs entirely on local hardware, built on free, open-source software. No datacenters. No subscriptions. No personal information leaving the building. It gives schools, seniors, patients, and people with disabilities the same access to capable AI as anyone else — at the cost of a computer, not a contract.

01 The problem

Today's AI is gated twice: your data goes away, and the price to keep it keeps rising.

The privacy toll

Cloud AI only works by sending every question, document, and record to a private datacenter. For a frail elder's health notes, a student's file, or a disabled person's care plan, that transfer is a compliance problem before it's a technical one — and an ethical one before it's a compliance problem.

The access toll

Useful AI lives behind per-seat subscriptions and datacenter budgets. The institutions that serve the people who need AI most — underfunded schools, care homes, hospitals, disability services — are the ones least able to pay that toll. The gap widens by default.

The hardware toll

Running AI locally is honest work: these machines still draw serious power, and the hardware itself is getting more expensive. Datacenter build-outs are buying up the world's memory supply, and the resulting RAM shortage has pushed the cost of a capable machine up for everyone. That inflated cost of entry is precisely what this proposal exists to fight.

02 The Warden approach

Keep the data. Keep the model. Run it in the room.

Warden runs the AI model on the same machine it serves. The software is free and open-source; the only cost is the computer it runs on. Because nothing leaves the device, the populations who need the most protection get the most protection by default — not by policy, by architecture.

04 The model

Public funds the hardware. The software is free. Training sustains it.

01

Government funds installation for the needy

Public procurement buys and installs the local computers — on the order of $100,000 machines with the memory and power to run capable AI — in schools, care homes, hospitals, and disability services. The state owns the hardware; the institution owns its data. Free AI for the people inside, permanently.

Public sectorfunds hardware + install
02

The software is given away free

Warden is free, open-source software. No license fees to the institution, no per-seat cost to the taxpayer beyond the one-time hardware. Any school or facility can run it, audit it, or extend it.

Open source$0 license, forever
03

Education is sold to those who can pay

Private training on how to actually use AI tools — sold to districts, businesses, and individuals who can afford it — funds the work and the free deployment to those who can't.

Private marketfunds the mission
05 The endgame

Once it's sustained, the whole thing goes open.

The education revenue exists to buy time — enough to mature the platform and put more into a more profitable ambition. At that point Warden's full design and source are released to the public for free. The state, the schools, and the public own the infrastructure they paid for, outright.

06 The ask

Install local AI in the rooms that need it most.

Step 1

Pilot Warden in a small set of classrooms and one care facility. One capable machine per site. Measure use, privacy posture, power draw, and cost.

Step 2

Procure $100,000-class machines at scale for the institutions serving students, patients, elders, and disabled residents. Free software, no per-seat fees — the hardware is the whole cost.

Step 3

Fund ongoing training so staff can use the tools well — the revenue stream that keeps the free deployment self-sustaining.
